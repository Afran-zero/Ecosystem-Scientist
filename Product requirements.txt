Product requirements: Ecosystem Scientist
The product should be a playable, browser-based, pixel-art ecosystem game with one explorable island, one functional research laboratory, and exactly three restoration missions.
The open world is where the player observes and interacts with animals. The laboratory is a conventional data interface: clickable controls, sliders, numeric inputs, charts, diagrams, population bars, and experiment results. It does not need decorative laboratory illustrations.
The three missions use separate island states to represent different environmental problems. They share the same simulation engine, but each starts from a distinct damaged ecosystem and has its own objectives and restoration criteria.

Screen behavior
Main menu: Continue restores the saved state; New Game initializes a fresh save; Missions opens the mission selection screen.
Island: the player moves freely within map boundaries, collides with terrain and objects, and interacts with nearby organisms and buildings.
Research lab: opens as a dedicated screen or large overlay. The world simulation pauses while experiments are configured and analyzed.
Field journal: displays discovered species, observations, habitat information, and trophic relationships.
Mission results: shows whether objectives were satisfied, the measurements supporting that outcome, and a short scientific explanation.
Every screen must have a clear way back to the island. The player should not need to refresh the page or lose the current simulation to navigate between screens.
3. Open-world map requirements
The world must be a genuinely explorable, scrollable map—not a single screen with a small island image behind the player.

3.2 Required island regions
The hub island must contain:
Grassland: open space for rabbits, cows, and grazing behavior.
Forest: trees, shade, shelter, and fox habitat.
Pond: water access and the location for the pollution mission.
Meadow: the primary grass-regrowth and grazing area.
Forest edge: a transition zone where predators can encounter prey.
Research station: the entrance to the laboratory and mission selection interface.
Coastline: a natural boundary around the map.
The regions must be distinguishable through terrain tiles and vegetation, not merely by labels on the map.
3.3 Map interaction requirements
The player must be able to:
Move in four directions with WASD or arrow keys.
Move diagonally if the movement implementation supports it, without gaining extra speed.
Walk behind and in front of trees and buildings using correct sprite depth ordering.
Approach an animal and interact using E or a contextual interaction button.
Approach the research station and enter the lab.
Walk to a mission access point and open the mission briefing.
See a contextual prompt when an interactable object is within range.
Stop at map boundaries and avoid walking through solid objects.
Move the camera smoothly without the map shaking or blurring unnecessarily.
Pause world simulation while menus, the lab, and mission result screens are active.
The map must be rendered from individual tiles and sprites. Do not use one large generated image as the entire background, because it prevents reliable collision, interaction, terrain changes, and animal movement.
3.4 Mission maps
All missions should reuse the same tile assets, terrain definitions, collision rules, and animal systems.
Each mission loads its own map configuration and environmental state. The player should visibly see the damage before starting an experiment.
World state

Mission maps may share a common base map, but their environmental states must be independently stored. Completing a mission should not accidentally overwrite the starting conditions of another mission.

The player must be able to reset the experiment without erasing unrelated mission progress. Each experiment record should store its starting state, settings, random seed, duration, and resulting measurements.
5. Ecosystem simulation requirements
The simulation must be implemented as a separate system from the graphics. The animals shown on the island are visual representations of entities managed by the simulation engine.
This separation allows the lab to run experiments without relying on player movement or rendering speed.

5.3 Animal behavior state machine
Each animal uses a finite-state machine. The state transitions must be deterministic for a given simulation state and random seed.
stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> Idle
    Idle --> SearchFood: Hunger rises
    Idle --> Wander: No urgent need
    Wander --> SearchFood: Hunger rises
    SearchFood --> Eat: Food found
    SearchFood --> Wander: No food nearby
    Eat --> Rest: Energy restored
    Rest --> Wander: Rest complete
    Wander --> Flee: Predator detected
    SearchFood --> Flee: Predator detected
    Flee --> SearchFood: Threat leaves
    Eat --> Reproduce: Eligible and healthy
    Reproduce --> Wander: Reproduction complete
    Idle --> Dead: Death condition
    SearchFood --> Dead: Starvation
    Rest --> Dead: Death condition
The state machine must be species-aware:
Rabbits prioritize grass, nearby shelter, and escape from foxes.
Cows prioritize accessible grass and water.
Chickens and mice forage for their configured resources.
Foxes select reachable prey, chase it, and attempt a hunt.
Grass grows according to environmental conditions and available space.
Trees grow more slowly and are not consumed by grazing animals in the MVP.
Animals must not teleport to food or predators. Movement should use speed, distance, detection radius, and collision rules.
5.4 Simulation time
Use a fixed ecological time step of one in-game hour, with 24 steps per in-game day.
Within each step, process:
Environmental changes.
Plant growth and resource availability.
Animal energy and health.
Movement and target selection.
Feeding and hunting.
Births and deaths.
Population and habitat statistics.
Mission objective updates.
The rendering frame rate must not determine ecological outcomes. A slower computer should render fewer frames, not produce a different population trajectory.
The live island can advance in real time, but the laboratory should be able to fast-forward simulation time without rendering every intermediate frame.
5.5 Reproduction and population limits
Each species must have configurable parameters for:
Minimum reproductive age.
Reproduction probability.
Reproduction cooldown.
Minimum health and energy.
Gestation or incubation delay, if modeled.
Maximum population.
Mortality rate.
Maximum age or age-dependent mortality.
A birth should only occur when the configured conditions are met. Animals must not reproduce on every simulation step.
The MVP may model births as the appearance of a new individual after a reproduction event. It does not need detailed genetics or realistic gestation animations.
5.6 Vegetation model
Grass coverage must be represented as a value between 0 and 100 for each grassland region, with spatial patches controlling where grazing is possible.
The grass-growth function must depend on sunlight, water availability, soil quality, and pollution. Grazing removes available biomass, while regrowth restores it over time.
Trees should have their own count and growth state. Tree loss reduces forest cover and habitat availability.
The model must enforce resource limits: grass cannot grow above its maximum coverage, and grazing cannot consume more biomass than is available.
5.7 Food-web rules
Define the food-web relationships centrally in configuration, rather than hard-coding them separately into the animals, charts, and mission logic.
Example relationships:
Grass → rabbit
Grass → cow
Seeds → chicken
Seeds → mouse
Rabbit → fox
Mouse → fox
A feeding event must update the prey’s state, the predator’s energy, and the relevant statistics. A predator must not consume the same prey twice.
The simulation should support competition for food, prey scarcity, and declining populations. However, it does not need to reproduce every detail of a real biological ecosystem. The MVP is an educational model, so its assumptions should be documented and exposed to the player when relevant.
5.8 Reproducibility and validation
Every experiment must use a known initial state and a recorded random seed. Repeating the same experiment with the same configuration must reproduce the same result.
Required checks:
No negative population counts.
No negative health, energy, or grass biomass.
No duplicate animal IDs.
No births above the species population limit.
No consumption of unavailable resources.
No animals moving outside the island or through blocked terrain.
No NaN or infinite numerical values.
All chart data must agree with the underlying simulation state.
6. The three restoration missions
The product must contain exactly three missions. Each mission has a distinct damaged map, a fixed starting state, a briefing, an experimental task, measurable success criteria, and a completion report.
The missions must be completable in any order, provided their selection requirements are satisfied. The hub island remains available throughout.
Mission 1 — Restore the Grazing Meadow
Problem: The meadow has suffered severe vegetation loss. Rabbits and cows are competing for insufficient grass, and the animal population is becoming unsustainable.
Starting condition
Grass coverage: 25%.
Rabbit population: 24.
Cow population: 12.
Fox population: 4.
Trees: 18.
Rainfall: reduced from the hub baseline.
Soil quality: moderately degraded.
These are initial game parameters, not real-world ecological estimates. They must be configurable in the mission data.
Player objectives
Inspect the meadow and identify the low grass coverage.
Open the lab and review the population and vegetation charts.
Test at least one restoration intervention.
Run the ecosystem for a minimum of 30 simulated days.
Submit an experiment and explain the observed results.
Available experimental controls
Rainfall, sunlight, soil quality, initial herbivore populations, and experiment duration.
Completion criteria
Grass coverage reaches at least 60%.
Neither rabbits nor cows becomes extinct.
The ecosystem completes the required observation period.
The player submits a valid experiment record and evidence.
The player should not be forced to manipulate only one variable. Multiple strategies can pass if they satisfy the objective and the experiment record supports the result.
Educational assessment: carrying capacity, competition, plant growth, resource limitation, and population dynamics.

Problem: Forest clearing has reduced tree cover and fragmented the habitat used by foxes and mice. The player must restore sufficient habitat and determine whether the ecosystem can recover.

Explore the pond and inspect its environmental condition.
Review pollution, water quality, vegetation, and population data.
Test pollution reduction or another permitted restoration intervention.
Run the experiment for at least 30 simulated days.
Submit evidence explaining whether the ecosystem improved.
Available experimental controls
Pollution, rainfall, soil quality, sunlight, initial populations, and simulation duration.

Pollution falls to 30/100 or below.
Water quality reaches at least 65/100.

Pollution and water quality must be linked through the simulation rules. Reducing pollution should not instantly restore every organism; vegetation and populations should respond according to their own recovery dynamics.
Educational assessment: environmental pollution, ecosystem stress, causal reasoning, and restoration.
Shared mission rules
All missions must implement the same interaction contract:
The mission briefing states the problem, available variables, and success criteria.
A mission has a dedicated starting state and a reset action.
The player can inspect the map before opening the lab.
Experiments are stored separately from the mission’s original conditions.
Objectives update from simulation events and measured statistics.
Completion is calculated from explicit criteria, not from a hard-coded success button.
The results screen reports the initial state, intervention, duration, final state, and criteria satisfied.
Mission completion persists after refresh.
Replaying a mission starts from its defined initial state, not from a previously restored state.
The three missions should use shared code and configuration-driven data. Do not build three separate versions of the simulation.

8. Data contracts and simulation configuration
The core systems must share a defined data model.
Animal entity
interface Animal {
  id: string;
  speciesId: string;
  x: number;
  y: number;
  ageHours: number;
  health: number;
  energy: number;
  hunger: number;
  state: AnimalState;
  targetId?: string;
  reproductionCooldownHours: number;
  alive: boolean;
}
AnimalState should be a union of supported behavior states, such as idle, wander, searchFood, eat, hunt, flee, rest, and dead.
Environment state
interface EnvironmentState {
  temperatureC: number;
  rainfallMmPerDay: number;
  sunlightPercent: number;
  soilQuality: number;
  pollution: number;
  waterQuality: number;
  simulationHour: number;
}
Define units and valid ranges centrally. UI values and simulation values must use the same units.
Mission definition
interface MissionDefinition {
  id: string;
  title: string;
  mapId: string;
  initialStateId: string;
  durationDays: number;
  objectives: MissionObjective[];
  allowedControls: string[];
}
Each objective should specify a measurable condition and whether it must hold at the end of the run or throughout the run. This prevents ambiguity when evaluating mission success.
Experiment record
interface ExperimentRecord {
  id: string;
  missionId: string;
  seed: number;
  durationDays: number;
  startingState: SimulationSnapshot;
  interventions: ExperimentIntervention[];
  finalState: SimulationSnapshot;
  statistics: SimulationStatistics;
  createdAt: string;
}
Snapshots must contain all state necessary to reproduce the run, including animal states, vegetation, environment, simulation time, and random-generator state. A seed alone is insufficient if the experiment starts from a previously evolved ecosystem.
9. Save and recovery requirements
The MVP should save locally in the browser.
Persist:
Selected mission and current map.
Player position.
Current live simulation state.
Discovered species and journal entries.
Mission completion status.
Completed experiment records.
User interface preferences where appropriate.
Save schema version.
The save service must validate stored data before loading it. If a save is corrupted or has an unsupported version, the game must present a recovery option instead of crashing.
Save at meaningful checkpoints: after mission completion, after submitting an experiment, after changing the live world, and periodically during play. Avoid writing the entire save on every rendered frame.
A New Game action must explicitly confirm that existing progress will be replaced.

11. Implementation order and delivery milestones
The build must proceed in dependency order. The ecosystem simulation should be validated before all three missions are populated with content.
Milestone 1 — Playable world
 Deliver the expanded island, player movement, camera, collisions, map transitions, and asset loading. The map must be scrollable and interactive.
Milestone 2 — Living ecosystem
 Deliver the seven species, food web, movement, feeding, hunting, reproduction, mortality, vegetation growth, and a deterministic simulation clock.
Milestone 3 — Research laboratory
 Deliver the complete lab layout, functional controls, simulation snapshots, experiment execution, charts, and baseline comparisons.
Milestone 4 — Three missions
 Deliver the meadow, forest, and pond scenarios, with their individual initial states, objectives, evaluation logic, and results screens.
Milestone 5 — Persistence and quality assurance
 Deliver local saves, recovery behavior, automated tests, input validation, performance checks, and a deployable production build.
Final product boundary
The end-to-end MVP consists of one shared island engine, three damaged mission scenarios, seven organism types, a functional scientific dashboard, reproducible experiments, measurable restoration objectives, and persistent progress.
Do not expand the scope beyond these requirements until the full loop—from entering the island to completing and saving all three missions—works reliably.

Download the ZIP files, inspect the PNG spritesheets, and retain each pack’s license file or license information in your project repository. CC0 packs are particularly convenient because they generally allow commercial use without mandatory attribution.
